    Practising SQL Server for the requests you actually get

    Work doesn’t arrive one keyword at a time. It arrives as a message from someone who needs a number before a meeting: last week’s orders over £50, customers who haven’t bought anything, revenue by month. The hard part is turning that into the right columns, rows and date range, then trusting the answer enough to send it.

    Each ticket is a short email from a colleague at Harbour & Co, a made-up company with Customers, Orders, OrderLines, Products, Returns and Staff. You write T-SQL against a real database and send the result, and it’s checked against a known right answer. Marking is strict: SELECT *, an extra column or a missing ORDER BY gets sent back with a short note. There are 46 tickets of about five minutes each across seven simulated workdays.

    The tickets cover the problems that come up at the desk: date ranges that leak today’s orders (see last week’s orders), joins that lose rows or double your totals, WHERE vs HAVING, and report queries with CASE and ROW_NUMBER.

    It’s for analysts and new starters who use SQL Server Management Studio but feel slow with it, people moving from Excel (the Excel to SQL converters show VLOOKUP as a JOIN and pivots as GROUP BY), and anyone preparing for a data interview with the interview practice page. If you just need to get something done, the free T-SQL tools and guides are open to everyone.
