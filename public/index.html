    Why practise this way

    Practising SQL Server for the requests you actually get

    Work doesn’t arrive one keyword at a time. It arrives as a message from someone who needs a number before a meeting: last week’s orders over £50, customers who haven’t bought anything, revenue by month. The hard part is turning that into the right columns, rows and date range, then trusting the answer enough to send it. WorkSQL is practice for exactly that.

    What a ticket is

    One short request from a colleague at Harbour & Co, a made-up company with the tables most small businesses have: Customers, Orders, OrderLines, Products, Returns and Staff. You pick your stack (SQL Server, PostgreSQL or MySQL), read the email, write the query in a query window, run it against a real database and send the result back. Each one takes about five minutes.

    How it’s marked

    Every result is checked against a known right answer, and the marking is strict on purpose. SELECT *, an extra column, a wrongly named header, rows in the wrong order or a missing ORDER BY all get sent back with a short note from the senior DBA. Hints point at the mistake; they never paste the query.

    How the week is laid out

    46 tickets across seven simulated workdays, about six a day after Monday. The first time you meet something new, like CASE, LEFT JOIN or a CTE, it’s a step-by-step walkthrough; then two reps on your own. Monday’s ten tickets are free, with no sign-up.

    The problems that come up at the desk

    Date ranges that leak this morning’s orders (see last week’s orders and GETDATE). Joins that lose rows, or double your totals. WHERE vs HAVING and the GROUP BY error. Report queries with CASE, month grouping and ROW_NUMBER.

    Who it’s for

    Analysts and new starters who use SQL Server Management Studio but feel slow with it. People moving from Excel, where the Excel to SQL converters show VLOOKUP as a JOIN and pivots as GROUP BY. And anyone preparing for a data interview, with top N per group, running totals and duplicates on the interview practice page.

    Free to use alongside

    If you just need to get something done, the free T-SQL tools run in your browser: a formatter, a DATEADD helper, a CASE builder, mock INSERTs and more. The guides answer the questions people search for at work, and the T-SQL reference covers the functions you’ll use most.
